### TM theory and dynamics
- Helin, Granmo, Shende, Jiao, Zadorozhny, Dumbre, Shafik, Yakovlev, 2025, "Uncertainty Quantification in the Tsetlin Machine", arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04175: the feedback equilibrium v = T(2P(y=1|x) - 1) (Eq. 6 and 7) reads class sums as a T-quantised probability (a one-output heuristic); prior art for any vote-to-probability map and for abstention. [verified in paper: full text]
- Sharma, Granmo, Jiao, 2022, "On the Equivalence of the Weighted Tsetlin Machine and the Perceptron", arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13634: with clauses held fixed, TM vote learning is perceptron learning, so ties with LR on the same literals are the expected case; it says nothing about clause composition. [verified in paper: abstract and conclusion]
- Belaid, Sharma, Jiao, Granmo, Andersen, Yazidi, 2023, "Generalized Convergence Analysis of Tsetlin Machines: A Probabilistic Approach to Concept Learning", arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02005: in the decoupled PCL setting each literal's inclusion is a thresholded frequency test, which supports count-seeded clauses as the no-training baseline. [verified in paper: full text]
- Gorji, Granmo, Phoulady, Goodwin, 2019, "A Tsetlin Machine with Multigranular Clauses", arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07310: builds varying specificity into each clause and removes the global s, a CAIR-native alternative to an s sweep for over-specific clauses. [verified: missed-paper entry added at the second check, marked verified; content as summarised there]
- Morris, Rafiev, Xia, Shafik, Yakovlev, Brown, 2022, "An Alternate Feedback Mechanism for Tsetlin Machines on Parallel Architectures", ISTM 2022, pp. 53 to 56, https://doi.org/10.1109/istm54910.2022.00018: convergence without the class sum by limiting included literals; prior art for class-sum-free local updates and for a literal cap. [verified in paper: abstract via OpenAlex, missed-paper entry added at the second check]
- Glimsdal, Saha, Bhattarai, Giri, Sharma, Tunheim, Yadav, 2022, "Focused Negative Sampling for Increased Discriminative Power in Tsetlin Machines", ISTM 2022, pp. 73 to 80, https://doi.org/10.1109/ISTM54910.2022.00021: draws the negative class in proportion to clip(T + v_c)/(2T) (tmu `focused_negative_sampling`), the CAIR comparator for frequency-drawn negatives over thousands of targets. [verified in paper: abstract; tmu source `coalesced_classifier.py`]
- Granmo, Andersen, Jiao, Zhang, Blakely, Berge, Tveit, 2023, "Learning Minimalistic Tsetlin Machine Clauses with Markov Boundary-Guided Pruning", ISTM 2023, pp. 1 to 8, https://doi.org/10.1109/istm58889.2023.10454914 (arXiv 2309.06315): Type III feedback prunes literals outside the Markov boundary during learning (tmu `type_iii_feedback`), Granmo's own remedy for over-specific clauses. [verified in paper: full text; corrected: venue ISTM 2023 and seven authors per the PDF and Crossref, arXiv lists six]

### TM for text and self-supervision
- Yadav, Jiao, Granmo, Goodwin, 2022, "Robust Interpretable Text Classification against Spurious Correlations Using AND-rules with Negation", IJCAI 2022, pp. 4439 to 4446, https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2022/616: the mechanism of negation drift on sparse bag-of-words input and CAIR's published counterfactual-IMDb result (E6, E7); s was chosen on the counterfactual test data, so its numbers are optimistic bounds. [verified in paper: full text]
- Kadhim, Granmo, Jiao, Shafik, 2024, "Exploring State Space and Reasoning by Elimination in Tsetlin Machines", ISTM 2024, pp. 1 to 8, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09162: low s with high T drives clauses to all-negated form (reasoning by elimination), so any s sweep must cap negation; its Table III varies s and T together. [verified in paper: full text]
- Sharma, Yadav, Granmo, Jiao, 2023, "Drop Clause: Enhancing Performance, Robustness and Pattern Recognition Capabilities of the Tsetlin Machine", AAAI 2023, 37(11), pp. 13547 to 13555, https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v37i11.26588 (arXiv 2105.14506 under the title "Drop Clause: Enhancing Performance, Interpretability and Robustness of the Tsetlin Machine"): a regulariser (tmu `clause_drop_p`) that cut epoch time and raised text accuracy in its setup; its quality effect here must be measured. [verified in paper: full text]
- Yadav, Bhattarai, Jana, Jiao, Yimam, 2024, "Pruning Literals for Highly Efficient Explainability at Word Level", ISTM 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04557: post-hoc removal of rare literals, a zero-training compaction for printed clauses and receipts. [verified in paper: full text; corrected: authors as on arXiv and in the PDF]
- Halenka, Kadhim, Clarke, Bhattarai, Saha, Granmo, Jiao, Andersen, 2024, "Exploring Effects of Hyperdimensional Vectors for Tsetlin Machines", arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02648: dense hypervector literals make negations informative and group features by collision; an encoding against negation drift. [verified in paper: full text]
- Berge, Granmo, Tveit, Goodwin, Jiao, Matheussen, 2019, "Using the Tsetlin Machine to Learn Human-Interpretable Rules for High-Accuracy Text Categorization with Medical Applications", IEEE Access 7, pp. 115134 to 115146, https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04547: origin of the "TM on par with linear bag-of-words baselines" claim; on small noisy data LR and NB tie with it (prior for H17.11). [verified in paper: full text of arXiv v2]

### Hierarchy, logic networks and learnability
- Valiant, 1984, "A Theory of the Learnable", Communications of the ACM 27(11), pp. 1134 to 1142, https://doi.org/10.1145/1968.1972: Theorem A learns k-CNF from positive examples by elimination and the dual learns bounded DNF from negatives; the flat TM with negations recovered H-AO in this De Morgan form, as "group absent" clauses (pooled Jaccard 0.81 to 0.86 [measured, exploratory: `results/20260925T035032Z_ssl_r2_hao_head_r1_hier_exploratory/` and its five siblings]). [verified in paper: full text]
- Pitt, Valiant, 1988, "Computational Limitations on Learning from Examples", Journal of the ACM 35(4), pp. 965 to 984, https://doi.org/10.1145/48014.63140: proper learning of read-once formulas is worst-case hard unless R = NP; the hardness is representation-dependent and GBDT solves H-AO, so it does not excuse the package's failure. [verified in paper: abstract]
- Malach, Shalev-Shwartz, 2019, "Learning Boolean Circuits with Neural Networks", arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11923: the local correlation assumption for layer-wise learning of tree circuits; it assumes known wiring and product inputs, so it fits H-AO only loosely. [verified in paper: full text]
- Malach, Shalev-Shwartz, 2019, "Is Deeper Better only when Shallow is Good?", NeurIPS 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03488: conjecture that gradient-based learning of deep models succeeds mainly where shallow models already do well (H14.2, H17.5). - Abbe, Boix-Adsera, Brennan, Bresler, Nagaraj, 2021, "The staircase property: How hierarchical structure can guide deep learning", NeurIPS 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10573: hierarchies are learnable by greedy layer-wise rules when lower-order signal forms a staircase; motivates H-AO worlds with graded leaf correlation. [verified in paper: abstract]
- Abbe, Boix-Adsera, Misiakiewicz, 2022, "The merged-staircase property: a necessary and nearly sufficient condition for SGD learning of sparse functions on two-layer neural networks", arXiv preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08658: the necessary-condition companion of the staircase result. - Abbe, Boix-Adsera, Misiakiewicz, 2023, "SGD learning on neural networks: leap complexity and saddle-to-saddle dynamics", COLT 2023, PMLR 195, pp. 2552 to 2623, https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11055: learning time grows with the leap of the target, and H-AO has no degree-1 signal (an analogy for TA feedback). [verified in paper: abstract; corrected: venue COLT 2023]
- Abbe, Bengio, Lotfi, Rizk, 2023, "Generalization on the Unseen, Logic Reasoning and Degree Curriculum", ICML 2023 (extended JMLR version), https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13105: H-AO's test is an unseen-combination split, so seen and unseen accuracy must be reported separately.
