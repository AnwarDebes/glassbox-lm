# Results of the tm_language_model study

The tm_language_model study ran from 19 September to 1 October 2026 and is archived. Its question was whether a Tsetlin Machine can be the core of a token-level language model trained at corpus scale with a reported likelihood, on a sparse event-driven engine written for that purpose, with hypotheses and thresholds registered before the runs ([docs/13-preregistration.md](13-preregistration.md)). The reproducibility hypotheses were met at the scale tested: a bootstrap from a clean copy, bit-exact replay, hash equality of the CPU and GPU engines, generation receipts that pass a standalone checker, and bit-exact clause sharding on the GPU. The main accuracy goal was missed. The anchor run (causal, V = 8000, 1,048,576 clauses, seed 1) scores 2.2193 bits per byte on the WikiText-103 test split against 1.3719 for the in-house Kneser-Ney 5-gram on the same tokens, on a single seed, with one read of the test split and with 0.114 of the test 8-grams also present in the training split [measured: `results/20260923T042350Z_CP_anchor/metrics.json`], so H1.1 is not met on seed 1 and the registered kill criterion KB7 triggered; seeds 2 and 3 were not run. The stock Graph Tsetlin Machine did not beat the left bigram (H5.1), the calibration gate was missed, incremental re-evaluation was slower than the engine's forward pass (H6.4), and the scaling experiments and four of the five speed hypotheses were never run. Further hypothesis families (H8 to H17) were then registered by dated amendments. Among them a count-vote read-out met H8.1, a threshold that compares it with the signed-vote read-out of the same clause bank and not with the n-gram baseline, and the confirmatory self-supervised tests were negative: H17.1 to H17.5, H17.10 and H17.11 were not met, and H17.12 was met.

## How to read this page

Every registered id appears once, with one status: met, not met, not judged (runs exist, and no verdict was drawn from them) or not run (no run of the registered measurement exists that can be used; flagged runs do not count). The gates of amendment A4 and the decision rows of amendment A5 are the exception: they are branching rules without a pass condition, and each appears once with its outcome (applied, not applied, not reached or not decided). A kill criterion is triggered or not triggered; a kill criterion that was never evaluated is listed as not judged. A qualifier next to a status (on seed 1 only, at tiny-LM scale, post hoc, exploratory) belongs to the verdict. The verdicts are those noted when each run was judged; the working notes that hold them are not part of the repository. Two exceptions were read on 2026-10-25 from complete registered runs for which no verdict had been recorded at the time, and are marked as such: H8.11 (met) and part (a) of H8.8 (met; H8.8 as a whole stays not judged).

Every measurement is followed by a label of the form `[measured: results/<run id>/<file>]`, which names the file the value was read from, with the path relative to the study folder. Run folders and datasets are not distributed. A reader of a clone can compare this page with the registration, the code and the run configurations, and cannot recompute a number without running the experiment again. Runs that are flagged in the [run index](../../../docs/run-index.md) at the repository root (never cite, stale, killed, smoke, biased exploratory) are not used as results; some of them are named on this page, without their numbers. Verdicts that rest on a single seed have that as a qualifier.

## Hypotheses H1.1 to H1.4: accuracy (pillar P1)

The centre of this family is the anchor run `20260923T042350Z_CP_anchor`: the causal engine at V = 8000 with 1,048,576 clauses, seed 1, trained for 4 epochs over the 135,246,414 training tokens of WikiText-103 and scored in bits per byte by protocol CP ([docs/05-experiments.md](05-experiments.md)). On the test split, read once after the calibration had been fixed on validation, the model scores 2.2193 bits per byte and the in-house modified Kneser-Ney 5-gram (KN-5) on the same tokens scores 1.3719, a ratio of 1.618 [measured: `results/20260923T042350Z_CP_anchor/metrics.json`; the ratio is labelled derived in the run file]. The run's own flag for H1.1 (model below KN-5) is false, and its flag for KB7 (model above 1.5 times KN-5) is true. On validation the model scores 2.2234, 2.1907, 2.1913 and 2.1749 bits per byte after epochs 1 to 4, against 1.3501 for KN-5; no plateau is claimed. Causal top-1 on test is 0.1274 from the calibrated scores and 0.1360 from the raw class sums, against 0.3490 for KN-5. The per-word perplexity on test, converted from the subword stream and open-vocabulary, is 491.8 for the model and 46.1 for KN-5 [measured: `results/20260923T042350Z_CP_anchor/metrics.json`; both perplexities are labelled derived in the run file].

These numbers come with four conditions. They are single-seed: seeds 2 and 3 of the three registered seeds were not run. The reason given in the records of the study is a rule, fixed before the run, to stop after seed 1 if KB7 triggered. That rule is not in the published files: the registration, docs/08, the experiment configurations and the launch scripts do not contain it, and the working notes that state it are not part of the repository. The configuration, which lists seeds 1 to 3, was not changed. The test read is access number 22 on the study's access counter. The fraction of test 8-grams that also occur in the training split is 0.114 [measured: `results/20260923T042350Z_CP_anchor/metrics.json`]; the registered analysis plan (docs/13-preregistration.md, section 5) also asks for every test number on the de-overlapped subset, and the metrics file of the run holds no such number. The run held the shared GPU lock and its energy notes say that other jobs were on the device, so its training times per epoch (11,951 s, 12,239 s, 13,018 s and 18,725 s) are upper bounds.

A miss of H1.1 leads to the KB7 path of docs/08. The anchor number is therefore a first measurement with no superiority claim. The wide vote tier of that path was tried only in exploratory runs on a 5M-token slice (seed 101), for which no run folder exists; it was not run on the anchor configuration, and docs/08 marks that step as superseded by docs/12 F19.

H1.4 is judged on the tiny language model: masked mode, V = 2000, 65,536 clauses, 4,999,168 training tokens, 56,210 frozen validation targets. After epoch 4 the five registered seeds score top-1 0.1891, 0.1865, 0.1883, 0.1877 and 0.1878; on the same positions the left bigram scores 0.1447 and the count probe 0.2002. KN-5 with both sides visible, counted on the same training slice, scores 0.4332 on these targets [measured: `results/20260922T225226Z_E1_comparators/metrics.json`], far above the model and the count probe. The lowest ratio to the count probe is 0.931 (seed 2), against a threshold of 0.70 [measured: `results/20260922T190301Z_tiny_lm/metrics.json`, `results/20260922T193047Z_tiny_lm/metrics.json`]. The engine settings of these runs were chosen after two logged attempts on seed 1 had failed, from exploratory runs on a 300k-token slice with seed 101 (the three sections below). The choice is post hoc with respect to those failures, and all attempts were scored on the same validation targets. The registration lists H1.4 under experiment s4, the deviation ladder of docs/05, while the judged runs are those of the tiny-LM experiment. The two runs of the ladder were killed, are flagged in the run index and have empty metrics files, so the ladder holds no result for H1.4.

H1.2 and H1.3 have no confirmatory run. For H1.2 the run index holds one exploratory slice run, `20260923T203943Z_E3_v8k_slice_exploratory`, and no run of the registered three-seed configuration at V = 8k; no verdict was drawn. For H1.3 the equal-cost transformer run (TB) is not in the run index. A sweep of small causal transformers of 2026-09-22, `20260922T172902Z_M2_baselines_transformer`, prepared the baseline one day before the anchor run and was not trained under the wall-clock and joule caps of that run.

### Tiny LM, first run

The first logged run of the tiny language model, `20260922T170141Z_tiny_lm` (seed 1, 6 epochs, the engine defaults of that day), scores top-1 0.0487, 0.0367, 0.0373, 0.0404, 0.0371 and 0.0360 after epochs 1 to 6, against 0.1447 for the left bigram and 0.2002 for the count probe on the same 56,210 targets [measured: `results/20260922T170141Z_tiny_lm/metrics.json`]. A unigram predictor scores 0.0341 on these targets [measured: `results/20260922T225226Z_E1_comparators/metrics.json`], so the model sits at the unigram level and below the bigram. KB2 failed on this run and its first branch applied: stop and diagnose. The diagnosis points at the focused negative rule R3 of [docs/11-engine-spec.md](11-engine-spec.md): on every example it selects the highest-scoring wrong classes, which under a Zipf-distributed target are the frequent classes nearly every time, so their votes are pushed down on almost every example and reinforced only when they are the target.

### KB2 diagnosis, third round

The second logged attempt, `20260922T182517Z_tiny_lm` (seed 1, 4 epochs, with recency weighting of candidates, binomial negatives and 64 vote slots), scores 0.1303, 0.1364, 0.1352 and 0.1330 after epochs 1 to 4: still below the bigram, so KB2 was not met [measured: `results/20260922T182517Z_tiny_lm/metrics.json`]. The third round of diagnosis was exploratory (300k-token slice, seed 101, 2 epochs); no run folder exists for it, and no number from it is reported. Its reading, which section 7 of docs/11 adopts, is that text needs many general clauses that stay stable (a high s), a seeding rate that fills the clause bank, recency-weighted candidates, and negatives drawn by frequency instead of from the top of the class sum.

The third logged attempt, `20260922T190301Z_tiny_lm` (seed 1, with s = 128, K_COVER = 8, SEED_CAP = 32 and RHO_SEED = 1 in addition), scores 0.1841, 0.1848, 0.1868 and 0.1891 after epochs 1 to 4, above the bigram and at 0.945 of the count probe: KB2 was met on seed 1 [measured: `results/20260922T190301Z_tiny_lm/metrics.json`]. The run files show the seeding difference directly: the second attempt ends with 36,377 of 65,536 clauses alive [measured: `results/20260922T182517Z_tiny_lm/metrics.json`], the third has all 65,536 alive after every epoch. Seeds 2 to 5 followed in `20260922T193047Z_tiny_lm` and gave the H1.4 verdict above.

It scores top-1 0.0644, 0.0487 and 0.0385 after epochs 1 to 3 [measured, exploratory: `results/20260922T174727Z_tiny_lm_diag_noneg/metrics.json`]: above the first run after epoch 1, falling afterwards, and below the left bigram throughout. ## Hypotheses H2.1 to H2.4: reproducibility and extensibility (pillar P2)

H2.1 asks for one bootstrap command with exit status 0 from a clean checkout, with its output logged. The criterion was judged on the bootstrap run of 2026-09-22 in `results/_bootstrap/20260922T145118Z_bootstrap` (cited in [docs/04-implementation-plan.md](04-implementation-plan.md)). The verdict has two limits: the repository had no commit, so the clean checkout was a copy of the working tree without environments, results, data and caches; and the package cache already held every wheel and the pycuda build, so the run says nothing about download or compile time. The environment check made in that copy reports 0 failed probes and 8 successful imports [measured: `results/20260922T145432Z_envcheck_clean_copy/metrics.json`]. The numbers lint test fails in such a copy, because it requires the cited run folders to exist. The bootstrap was run again in the same copy after the tmu pin of the same day (section below), and exit status 0 was noted for that run as well. The bootstrap folders are not part of the run index.

H2.2 and H2.4 were judged on `20260922T232251Z_E11`, which retrains the tiny-LM engine for 4 epochs and generates 32 new tokens from each of 20 prompts of 16 tokens, the starts of the first 20 test documents (a counted test access), with three decoders. For H2.2 the receipts of an any-order generation (seed 1, first 3 prompts) have the same SHA-256 in process and in two subprocesses with 1 and 2 OpenMP threads. For H2.4, 11,400 of 11,400 receipts pass the standalone checker, which runs as a separate process, and 1,113 of 1,113 tampered receipts fail, over 12 tampering modes [measured: `results/20260922T232251Z_E11/metrics.json`]. Both verdicts hold at tiny-LM scale. The tampering count has a limit. The checker recomputes the random draw of a sampled token from the seed, the stream and the counters that the receipt itself records; an internal audit of 2026-10-03 (judged by an AI model, not a person) found that the checker does not tie them to the seed of the generation run or to the position of the token, and that it accepted a receipt with a forged draw ([known limitations](../../../docs/known-limitations.md), section 4). On 2026-10-25 the checker was changed to bind every draw to the run, the decoding step and the position, and the mode and the temperature to the run (item 6 of its docstring). Three forgery modes were added to the tamper tests: a forged draw, a changed temperature and a sampled token declared greedy. The checker before the change accepts all three, and the changed checker rejects them (`tests/test_certify.py`). The re-check of the saved E11 receipts with the changed checker ran on 2026-10-25 under amendment A11 of the pre-registration (frozen at 15:04 UTC; run `20261025T150507Z_h2_4_recheck`): every input equal to its literal pin, the twelve old tamper counts reproduced, 11,400 of 11,400 receipts pass the bound checker, and 1,412 of 1,412 tampered receipts fail over 15 modes, among them 100 forged draws, 99 changed temperatures and 100 sampled tokens declared greedy; every one of these forgeries passes the checks of the E11 checker and fails only on the binding, in each of the three decoders. By A11's outcome table H2.4 and Gate 3 are re-judged met and the forged-draw qualifier is lifted. What a passing receipt shows: it is bound to its run's seed, temperature, decoder mode and step order; the windows are not chained to the earlier claims, and the run's final tokens are not compared with the claims (checker docstring). A recount of the receipts from the per-file reports in the run folder gives the same numbers (judged by an AI model, not a person).

The training half of H2.2 (equal state hash across launch configurations) and H2.3 (hash equality of the CPU and GPU engines after 100 steps at B in {64, 1024, 4096}) were judged on 2026-09-22 from a run of `tests/test_cuda_engine_parity.py`. No run folder holds the output of that test run, so the two verdicts are carried here without a measurement. One logged run touches the same property on short configurations: in `20260922T233629Z_m4_cuda_sanitizer` the inner comparison of hashes and counters with the CPU engine is recorded as passed under each of the four sanitizer tools [measured: `results/20260922T233629Z_m4_cuda_sanitizer/metrics.json`]. The run itself has exit status 1 in the run index: its `all_clean` flag is false, because memcheck counts 727 leak records. When the run was judged they were attributed to the memory pool of CuPy under the sanitizer; the baseline of the same run, without the engine, counts 24.

## Hypotheses H4.1 to H4.3: the proposal rows

H4.1 and H4.2 concern the hierarchical Tsetlin machine package on the paragraph-pair task of the supervisor's project proposal (unpublished). The registered experiment (five seeds, validation then test) has no run. On 2026-10-01, 27 exploratory probe runs on pair data were made in this study's results folder; they are flagged in the run index because the pair data had a sampling bias, and none of their numbers is used. The question was taken up by the [hierarchical_tm study](../../hierarchical_tm/README.md).

### H2 row d, seed 1

Row d of experiment H2 (docs/05-experiments.md) puts a second engine, in sparse tabular mode, on top of the fired-clause bits of a trained tiny-LM layer 1 and asks whether layer 2 improves on layer 1 (H4.3). On seed 1 of `20260923T125642Z_H2_row_d`, layer 1 reaches top-1 0.1891, the value of the H1.4 run of the same seed; layer 2 reaches 0.0157 with 231 alive clauses; the argmax of the summed scores of both layers reaches 0.1879; layer 2 minus layer 1 is -17.35 points [measured: `results/20260923T125642Z_H2_row_d/metrics.json`]. Over the five seeds layer 2 scores 0.0157, 0.0192, 0.0212, 0.0174 and 0.0166, the difference ranges from -17.35 to -16.71 points with a mean of -16.99, the paired one-sided Wilcoxon p is 1.0, and the run's own flag for H4.3 is false [measured: `results/20260923T125642Z_H2_row_d/metrics.json`].

This was not read as a verdict on a greedy second layer: layer 2 did not learn, because the sparse tabular mode itself does not learn. A training example carries about 69 fired layer-1 bits [measured: `results/20260923T125642Z_H2_row_d/metrics.json`], and the printed layer-2 clauses of seed 1 hold one literal each (lines of the form `IF clause_3310 THEN token_260`). In tabular mode every literal sits in segment 0, so the duplicate-segment rule of seeding leaves a seed clause with a single randomly drawn bit, which rarely fires again (docs/11, section 8, open question 8). H4.3 is therefore not judged, and the registered comparison was postponed until the layer-2 learner is changed. The run covers the tiny-LM part of H4.3 only; the paragraph-task part has no run.

## Hypotheses H5.1 to H5.3: kill-criterion checks on the stock package

These three ids restate kill criteria of the initial design for the stock Graph Tsetlin Machine package.

For H5.1 the six cells of the depth-2 sweep over s (10, 30, 100, 300, 1000 and 3000; seed 1; V = 2000; one epoch of 749,959 masked examples each) score top-1 between 0.0233 and 0.0421 on the 56,210 frozen validation targets [measured: `results/20260922T232858Z_E1_d2_s10_e1/metrics.json`, `results/20260923T002641Z_E1_d2_s30_e1/metrics.json`, `results/20260923T011847Z_E1_d2_s100_e1/metrics.json`, `results/20260923T021042Z_E1_d2_s300_e1/metrics.json`, `results/20260923T030812Z_E1_d2_s1000_e1/metrics.json`, `results/20260923T045457Z_E1_d2_s3000_e1/metrics.json`]. ### Planted rules

The acceptance line of milestone M3 asks for at least 80% of the planted rules to be recovered. Three logged runs exist. The first, `20260922T152940Z_planted_rules`, used a faulty corpus generator and says nothing about the engine. The second, `20260922T153126Z_planted_rules` (corrected generator, the specification defaults of that day, 3 epochs), recovers 30, 25 and 20 of 200 rules on seeds 1 to 3 and scores top-1 0.463, 0.4425 and 0.3535 on held-out rule positions, against 0.2565, 0.226 and 0.237 for the left bigram: far below the gate [measured: `results/20260922T153126Z_planted_rules/metrics.json`].

The third, `20260922T165439Z_planted_rules` (8,192 clauses, uniform segment weighting, a cap of 2 on included negated literals, 10 epochs), recovers 167, 161 and 156 of 200 rules under the superset definition, a mean of 80.7%, and 118, 103 and 107 under the strict definition; top-1 on held-out rule positions is 0.9515, 0.9605 and 0.936 [measured: `results/20260922T165439Z_planted_rules/metrics.json`]. Under the superset definition a rule counts as recovered when an alive clause with its top vote on the consequent includes both premise literals; the strict definition also requires that clause to fire on every sampled position where the premises hold. The gate is met by the superset definition, on the mean over seeds (seed 3 alone is at 78%), and not by the strict one. The strict definition was the first operationalisation. Reading the gate on the superset definition was decided after the first runs had been seen and is post hoc, and the settings of the third run were chosen from exploratory runs on seed 101.

The finding that section 8b of docs/11 cites comes from those exploratory runs, for which no run folder exists: with the recency weights of candidate sampling (2^(8 - |o|) per exact segment) a rule whose premise sits at offset -8 is sampled 128 times less often than one at offset -1 [derived from the weight table], so seeds and insertions almost never reach it. The logged evidence is indirect: the second run, with the recency weights of the specification, misses the gate, and the third, with uniform weights and the cap on negated literals, meets it under the superset definition; the two runs differ in other settings as well (clause count, batch size, s, epochs). Uniform weighting suits the planted task, whose rules are placed uniformly over the offsets, and not text: the tiny-LM attempts returned to recency weighting (section "KB2 diagnosis, third round").

## Amendment A3: the count-vote Tsetlin Machine (H8.1 to H8.11)

Amendment A3 (2026-09-24) registers the Count-Vote Tsetlin Machine (CVTM), specified in section 11 of [docs/11](11-engine-spec.md). The signed-vote engine of sections 1 to 10 stays as it is, and A3 does not re-judge H1.1. The CVTM changes the read-out: votes are derived from exact integer counts of how often a clause fires together with each target, not from signed steps. It has three clause families. Suffix clauses are enumerated left contexts whose votes reproduce the interpolated Kneser-Ney 5-gram (KN-5) exactly. Document clauses are an in-context memory of the document so far. Learned clauses are the bank that the Tsetlin automata train. The CVTM-core is the first two families and holds no learned clause; the CVTM-full adds the third.

The thresholds of this family have to be read together with their comparators. H8.1, the stand-alone count read-out of a trained bank, is compared with the signed votes of the same bank, that is with the model's own vote read-out, and not with an n-gram baseline. H8.3 and H8.4 compare the CVTM-core with KN-5, but the core contains KN-5 as a component, so in the words of the registration "beats KN-5" says nothing about Tsetlin learning: the accuracy of the core comes from counting and from in-context copying written as clause votes. Only H8.1 and H8.10 credit the Tsetlin Machine with accuracy. Wherever a run file holds an n-gram reference, it is reported below next to the registered comparison.

### The count read-out evaluation of 2026-09-24

A3 was written after an evaluation that is post hoc and exploratory. It was not pre-registered, it tests no threshold, and its runs are the ones that A3 discloses as seen before the H8 thresholds were set. Seven runs of 2026-09-24 work on banks of the signed-vote engine: two that had already been trained, and a young bank that one control run trains itself. The probes freeze a bank, count the (clause, target) pairs exactly in one pass over a training prefix, and score several read-outs of those counts on the validation split; two companion runs mix further count features into the saved rows. Their configurations restrict them to the validation split; six of the seven add that the test split is never loaded, and the seventh only recomputes statistics from saved rows. These runs carry no dataset hashes. The question was whether the read-out of the CKM design of 2026-09-23, a recursive Witten-Bell estimate over the fired clauses ordered by specificity, improves on the signed votes, and whether the learned clauses add anything over plain n-gram contexts under the same estimator. The scripts are `experiments/ckm_posthoc_probe.py` and its causal variants and the two `experiments/tm_levers_*` scripts.

On the masked task the bank is the tiny-LM bank of seed 1 (65,536 clauses, saved by `results/20260923T125642Z_H2_row_d/`), with counts from 4,761,176 positions of its training slice and the 56,210 frozen validation targets. The signed votes of the bank reach top-1 0.1891. The CKM read-out reaches 0.0804, less than half of that. Count-derived votes in the engine's own sum-of-votes form, the log-linear read-out that became section 11.5 of docs/11, reach 0.2954; the fit of that read-out stopped at its iteration limit without converging. The count references on the same targets are 0.1447 for the left bigram, 0.2379 for KN-5 on the left context alone and 0.4315 for KN-5 on both sides [measured, exploratory: `results/20260924T001024Z_ckm_probe_masked_exploratory/`].

On the causal task the bank is the epoch-4 checkpoint of the anchor run `results/20260923T042350Z_CP_anchor/` (1,048,576 clauses), with counts from the first 5M training tokens and all 282,587 validation positions. The signed votes with their logged calibration give 2.1749 bits per byte. The CKM read-out gives 4.3210, and 15.4062 when each clause keeps only its 64 largest slots. The same estimator on exact left contexts of 1 to 5 tokens gives 1.8783, the level of a Kneser-Ney bigram (1.8768) although it sees contexts of up to five tokens. A normalised linear mixture of the clause count distributions gives 1.9112. That is 0.26 bits per byte better than the signed votes and still worse than KN-5 fitted on the same counting documents (1.7035) [measured, exploratory: `results/20260924T012544Z_ckm_probe_causal_exploratory/`].

Two control runs ask whether the learned clauses matter. A young bank of the same size, trained from the same configuration and stopped early by a rule fixed in the run configuration (98 percent of the clauses alive), is a better count key than the bank trained for four epochs: 1.8789 against 1.9112 bits per byte under the same linear read-out at 5M counting tokens. In a mixture that already holds KN-5, the exact n-gram contexts, in-document recurrence and a document unigram cache, adding the clauses of the trained bank lowers validation from 1.5611 to 1.5400 at 5M counting tokens and from 1.4627 to 1.4497 at 15M [measured, exploratory: `results/20260924T022628Z_ckm_probe_causal_controls_exploratory/`], and from 1.4362 to 1.4249 at 20M [measured, exploratory: `results/20260924T032913Z_ckm_probe_causal_controls_20M_exploratory/`]. The share of the learned clauses shrinks as the counts grow: 0.0211, 0.0129 and 0.0113 bits per byte [derived]. The young bank gives 1.5418 in the same mixture at 5M, within 0.002 of the trained bank. When a document n-gram cache is added on both sides at 5M, with weights cross-fitted over two folds of the validation documents, the mixture reaches 1.4963 without any learned clause and 1.4872 with them [measured, exploratory: `results/20260924T022949Z_tm_levers_trigger_control_causal_exploratory/`]. The cache alone is therefore worth more than the learned clauses were without it.

The feasibility run adds, at 5M counting tokens and on both sides, a document n-gram cache, a fitted log-linear pointer to earlier tokens of the document and class and skip templates. That mixture reaches 1.4845 without any learned clause and 1.4822 with them, a share of 0.0022 bits per byte for the learned clauses [measured, exploratory: `results/20260924T021631Z_tm_levers_feasibility_causal_exploratory/`; the difference is labelled derived in the run file]; the weight fits of both rows stopped at their iteration limit in both folds. The run file holds a third row, `kn5+ngcache+attn+tmpl`: the same added features on plain KN-5, with no learned clause, reach 1.4824 [measured, exploratory: same run]. Against that row, the best one without learned clauses, the mixture with learned clauses is ahead by 0.0002 bits per byte [derived], a tenth of the share given above. The seventh run, `results/20260924T035633Z_ckm_probe_causal_scale_compare_exploratory/`, recomputes paired statistics from the saved rows of the two control runs and is not quoted here.

The evaluation supports two statements, both exploratory. The CKM read-out fails: on both tasks it is worse than the signed votes it was meant to replace. Count-derived votes repair the model's estimator: on the masked task they score above KN-5 on the left context and below KN-5 on both sides, and on the causal task they remain worse than KN-5 and than a Kneser-Ney bigram. The learned clauses add little over count and cache features: within 0.002 bits per byte of what a barely trained bank of the same size adds, and in the feasibility run 0.0002 bits per byte over the best row without them. By the decision of 2026-09-24 the CKM design was not implemented, and the count-vote read-out was specified and registered ([docs/12](12-design-changes.md) F19). The confirmatory test of the read-out is H8.1.

## Amendments A7 to A10: logical self-supervised representations (H17.1 to H17.12)

## Launch order and interruptions

The confirmatory runs were started by chain scripts, of which `launch/chain_r1_confirm.sh`, `launch/retry_confirm_common.sh` and `launch/chain_r2r4_confirm.sh` are published (see [launch/README.md](../launch/README.md)). The facts below matter for reading the run ids of the family; the exit statuses are those of the [run index](../../../docs/run-index.md).

The five confirmatory R1 chains (common, seed1, seed2, seed3, report) were started on 2026-09-25. A9 item 11 makes them wait until the exploratory control-study and configuration-arm chains have finished, and the chain tests this by looking for a completion line in the output of those chains. The shells of the control-study chains had been stopped on 2026-09-25 and their remaining steps were run by replacement scripts, so that line could not appear. On 2026-09-26, after the exploratory steps had finished, the wait was released by hand through the release file that the chain provides for this case, and the chain wrote the release into its list of skipped and failed steps. The last exploratory control-study report, `20260926T204018Z_ssl_r1c_report_exploratory`, precedes the first confirmatory run, `20260926T204447Z_ssl_r1cf_testhash_confirmatory`. The report stage of the configuration arms had not run at that point; it ran on 2026-09-30 (`20260930T203828Z_ssl_r1ca_report_exploratory`), after the confirmatory R1 runs. The scripts of the exploratory chains were not retained and are not in `launch/`; the order of their runs can be read from the run ids only.

The host went down on 2026-09-27 shortly after 02:11 UTC and was back on 2026-09-28. Every chain stopped, and the chain scripts and their state files, which lived in a temporary directory, were lost. `launch/chain_r1_confirm.sh` is a copy recovered on 2026-09-28, with two scheduling changes named in its header: the state files live next to the script, and a step whose latest run of the same name has exit status 0 is not run again. The chains were restarted on 2026-09-28 at 20:33 UTC. The list of skipped and failed steps was lost with them and was reconstructed on 2026-09-28 from the lines that had been printed earlier, which may be incomplete, and from the run records. Nine counted test accesses had been made before the reboot (A3 on seeds 1 to 3, three tasks each); the access audit of the final report shows every pair of configuration and task accessed once, so none was repeated.
